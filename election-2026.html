How the estimates work, and their limits

Start. Each race begins from a starting margin and spread set by the author, informed by public race ratings and past results. It is judgment. The table is in this page's source.

Evidence. Each county's count is compared with its 2024 presidential margin and the swing the start expects. Counted votes are treated as a biased sample of that county's final margin, because early batches often lean by vote type. A county with little counted moves the estimate little.

Expected vote. State files don't say how many votes remain. The page assumes each county's total is its 2024 turnout times a statewide midterm share (CO 80%, IA 75%, CA 72%), so "share counted" is an estimate that can be wrong in either direction.

Across states. One shared error links the three states, and it can barely move until a real share of the vote is in. A state's estimate stays hidden until its polls close.

Not modeled. Vote-method patterns specific to a state, turnout surprises beyond the noise above, recounts, late-mail patterns over days, and anything outside these three states. California counts for weeks, so its early night numbers deserve extra caution.

Sources. Colorado Secretary of State and Iowa Secretary of State (Clarity results files), California Secretary of State (api.sos.ca.gov).
