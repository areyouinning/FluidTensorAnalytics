Paradox

How can drying potatoes halve their weight?

You have 100 kg of potatoes that are 99% water. You leave them out until they are 98% water. How much do they weigh now?

The answer is 50 kg

The dry matter is 1 kg, and drying does not change it. At 98% water, that 1 kg is the remaining 2%, so the total is 1 / 0.02 = 50 kg. Drag the slider from 99% to 98% and watch half the grid empty.

Why it feels wrong

A drop of one point in water sounds tiny. But look at what doubled: the dry share went from 1% to 2%. The dry matter did not change, so the whole pile has to halve for it to be twice as large a share. Near 100%, a small change in the water is a large change in what is left over.

One rule covers it

total = dry mass / (1 − water fraction). Check it with the picker: start at 99.9% and move the slider to 99.8%. The dry mass is 0.1 kg and the total falls from 100 kg to 0.1 / 0.002 = 50 kg. Same halving, from a different one-point-looking step. Starting at 90% water, it takes a drop all the way to 80% for the same halving: 10 / 0.2 = 50 kg.

This bites in real life wherever a figure sits near 100% or near zero: purity, survival rates, error rates. There, "one point" can mean a factor of two.
