PAUL'S GAME GARAGE

Games
For grown-ups. Marble Maze: touch the board where the marble should go (or tip the phone, where the browser allows it) and the board tips that way, up to 16°. The marble speeds up at 5/7 · g · sin θ, the same rolling physics as Ramp Race, and bounces off the walls. The goal is the hole with the yellow rim and flag. The plain dark holes sit only in squares off the route to the goal; rolling into one sends the marble back one square behind the furthest point it reached on the route, not to the start. Every maze is a fresh random 8 × 8 grid with six holes. Marble Hill: press the red button, then touch where the marble should go. At each of six gates one lane is blocked by a road barrier that bounces the marble back up the hill, one has some gems and one has at least two more. The marble rolls at 5/7 · g · sin 8° with light grass drag, settling near 2.8 m/s. Big Launch: pull the ball back in the slingshot and let go; the pull direction sets the angle and the pull length sets the speed, up to 10.6 m/s. The ball leaves the sling at bucket-rim height, so release and landing heights match and the range is v² · sin 2θ / g, longest at 45°. The far bucket needs close to full pull near 45°. The last three flight paths stay on screen as dots. Word Hill: the marble rolls through gates of letter blocks to spell a short sentence. Wrong letters bounce it back; an empty lane leaves that character out. Each correctly spelled word scores its letter count, and a capital first letter, every space, the period and every word right add a super bonus of 10. Tile Time: after each round the score becomes the target; + adds one tile and × adds a block of rows × columns, so 21 can be 5 × 4 + 1. Scores: gems on Marble Hill, points on Word Hill, squares on the maze route, the winning ramp's angle, and the three bucket distances added up on Big Launch. Rain to the Lake: real terrain from the Continental Divide and Mount Blue Sky down Clear Creek through Idaho Springs and Golden to Standley Lake, heights drawn 2.5× taller. Hold the cloud to make rain. Each drop's temperature comes from the thermometer (Golden's height) minus 6.5 °C per km of height: at 0.5 °C or colder it lands as snow, otherwise it runs downhill along computed flow paths. Warmth melts snow in proportion to temperature. Time is sped up, and the canal from Clear Creek to the lake is simplified. The flood is a simple bathtub estimate, not a hydraulic model: when more drops pass the gauge in Golden than the channel holds (170 a second, averaged over about 4 seconds), low ground beside Clear Creek near Golden fills to a stage of up to 8 m above the channel, and drains more slowly than it rises. Measured: the terrain (AWS Terrain Tiles, built from USGS 3DEP and other public elevation data). Modeled: flow paths, snow line, melt, flood. Heights on a 240 × 146 grid of about 270 m cells. Ramp Race: drag a ramp's knob to set its slope, tap a ball to swap big and small, press the red button. The steeper ramp always wins, and ball size makes no difference.
