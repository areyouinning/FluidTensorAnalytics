PAUL'S GAME GARAGE

Games
For grown-ups. How it fits together: Rain to the Lake is home. Paul is the rain cloud flying over the real Clear Creek map; holding the blue button rains, which uses the cloud's water. Each game has a land, a signpost at a real place on the map, and is played on the real terrain around that place (at 4 times the map's size, with heights drawn 0.7 times as tall, and the ground under the game levelled into a clearing). The two hill games are played at true size on a real ski slope: Echo Mountain's main run, where the lodge sits at the top by Squaw Pass Road and the run falls about 190 m to the north. A game round's score, plus the Tile Time or Word Time points after it, comes back as raindrops. Marble Maze: touch the board where the marble should go (or tip the phone, where the browser allows it) and the board tips that way, up to 16°. The marble speeds up at 5/7 · g · sin θ, the same rolling physics as Ramp Race, and bounces off the walls. The goal is the hole with the yellow rim and flag. The plain dark holes sit only in squares off the route to the goal; rolling into one sends the marble back one square behind the furthest point it reached on the route, not to the start. Every maze is a fresh random 8 × 8 grid with six holes. Marble Hill: press the red button, then touch where the marble should go: the finger pushes the marble sideways (up to 1.9 m/s²). At each of six gates one lane is blocked by a road barrier that bounces the marble back up the hill, one has some gems and one has at least two more. The 58 m course lies on the top of Echo Mountain's main run and drops 15 m. Measured: the ground, on a 4 m grid (AWS Terrain Tiles, zoom 15), and where the forest stands (a 10 m Sentinel-2 photo, October 2024). Physics: the marble feels 5/7 · g · sin θ of the real slope under it (between about 10° and 20° here) and the real side slope across it, so it drifts and Paul steers against that. Modeled: snow drag, chosen so the marble tops out near 3.4 m/s; the winter snow cover; the course itself. Big Launch: in 3D, seen from behind the slingshot. Drag back to pull: the pull length sets the speed (up to 10.6 m/s) and pulling sideways aims the other way, up to 40° left or right; the ▲ ▼ buttons set the launch angle in 5° steps. The ball leaves the sling at bucket-rim height, so release and landing heights match and the range is v² · sin 2θ / g, longest at 45°. The buckets sit at different distances and directions; the far one needs close to full pull near 45°. The last three flight paths stay on screen as dots. Word Hill: on the same run as Marble Hill, 25 m to the east, with more snow drag (about 2.3 m/s) to leave time to read. The marble rolls through gates of letter blocks to spell a short sentence. Wrong letters bounce it back; an empty lane leaves that character out. Each correctly spelled word scores its letter count, and a capital first letter, every space, the period and every word right add a super bonus of 10. Tile Time: after a round (taking turns with Word Time) the score becomes the target; × adds a block of rows × columns and + adds one tile. Every tile sits on a 10 × 10 multiplication grid, numbered along its sides, row by row at the width of the first block, and covered squares light up; after a win the tiles slide into the best way to make the number. so 21 can be 5 × 4 + 1. Points: each × block earns a point for every tile it makes, and every addition (each + tile, and each block joined onto what is already there) costs a point, so the most points always means the least adding. After a win, an explainer lists the ways to make the number with the fewest additions, found by checking every block up to 10 × 10. Scores: gems on Marble Hill, points on Word Hill, squares on the maze route, the winning ramp's angle, and the three bucket distances added up on Big Launch. Rain to the Lake: real terrain from the Continental Divide and Mount Blue Sky down Clear Creek through Idaho Springs and Golden to Standley Lake, heights drawn 2.5× taller. Hold the cloud to make rain. Each drop's temperature comes from the thermometer (Golden's height) minus 6.5 °C per km of height: at 0.5 °C or colder it lands as snow, otherwise it runs downhill along computed flow paths. Warmth melts snow in proportion to temperature. Time is sped up, and the canal from Clear Creek to the lake is simplified. The flood is a simple bathtub estimate, not a hydraulic model: when more drops pass the gauge in Golden than the channel holds (170 a second, averaged over about 4 seconds), low ground beside Clear Creek near Golden fills to a stage of up to 8 m above the channel, and drains more slowly than it rises. Measured: the terrain (AWS Terrain Tiles, built from USGS 3DEP and other public elevation data) and the photo draped over it (contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2024: the median of four nearly cloud-free Sentinel-2 passes from late August to early October 2024, clouds masked, about 31 m per pixel). Drag the ground to move the map, drag the cloud to move the cloud, pinch to zoom, twist to turn, slide two fingers up or down to tilt; the compass turns the map back to north. Modeled: flow paths, snow line, melt, flood. Heights on a 240 × 146 grid of about 270 m cells. Word Time: spell the picture by tapping letter blocks in order; one word for each point from the last game, up to three. A word with no misses scores its letter count, a word with misses scores 1; after two misses on a letter, the right one pulses. Ramp Race: drag a ramp's knob to set its slope, tap a ball to swap big and small, press the red button. The steeper ramp always wins, and ball size makes no difference.
