Riddles

Where should you stand in the circle?

n people stand in a circle, numbered 1 to n. Starting with person 1, every second person steps out: 2, then 4, then 6, and on around the circle, skipping anyone already out, until one person is left. Where should you stand to be the last one in?

Try it

Press Step to send one person out, or Play to watch the whole game. Change the number of people and look at who is left.

The survivors

For n = 1 to 16 the last person in is 1, 1, 3, 1, 3, 5, 7, 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 1. The answer drops back to 1 at every power of two (1, 2, 4, 8, 16) and climbs by 2 until the next one.

The rule

Write n = 2m + L, with 0 ≤ L < 2m. The survivor is 2L + 1. For n = 41: 41 = 32 + 9, so the survivor is 2·9 + 1 = 19.

The binary trick

Write n in binary and move the leading 1 to the end. 41 is 101001; moving the 1 gives 010011, which is 19.

Here is why. If n is a power of two, the first lap removes every second person and leaves exactly half the circle, with person 1 again first in line. So the survivor stays at 1. Otherwise, remove L people and the circle is down to a power of two, with the next person to step out at the start. That person was originally number 2L + 1. Chopping off the leading 1 leaves L; doubling it and adding 1 is the shift left that puts a 1 at the end.

I checked the formula against a direct simulation for every n from 1 to 1000, and the binary rotation against it for the same range. They agreed every time.

A process that looks as if it needs a full simulation has a one-line answer once you write the number in the right base.

Named after the historian Flavius Josephus, from a story in his account of the siege of Yodfat. The every-second-person version and its binary answer are standard results in recreational mathematics. The words and diagram are mine.
