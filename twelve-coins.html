Riddles

Can three weighings find the fake coin?

Twelve coins look identical, but one is fake and weighs slightly more or slightly less than the rest. You do not know which. You have a balance with two pans and no weights. Find the fake coin, and whether it is heavy or light, in three weighings.

Try it

Tap a coin to move it: off the scale, left pan, right pan. “H” and “L” show what a coin could still be.

Counting the answers

The fake is one of 12 coins, and it is heavy or light. That is 12 × 2 = 24 possible answers.

A weighing has three outcomes: left heavier, right heavier, or balanced. Three weighings can tell apart at most 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 situations. Since 24 fits under 27, it is just possible, with almost nothing to spare.

What that says about the first weighing

After one weighing, two more remain, so each outcome may leave at most 3 × 3 = 9 answers.

Weigh 4 against 4 and the outcomes leave 8, 8 and 8. Weigh 6 against 6 and they leave 12, 12 and 0, which is already too many. Weigh 3 against 3 and they leave 6, 6 and 12, again too many.

Why the later weighings work

A coin that sat on the heavy side can now only be heavy. One that sat on the light side can only be light. Coins that were off the scale in a balanced weighing are known to be genuine.

So a later weighing can mix “possibly heavy”, “possibly light” and known-genuine coins to split what remains into three roughly equal parts.

Each weighing is worth one three-way answer, and the riddle is solved by never wasting one.
