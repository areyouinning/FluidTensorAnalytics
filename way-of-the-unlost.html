Chapter I

An explainer built for Leonard Susskind, whose answer to Hawking's paradox this follows: the stretched horizon and black hole complementarity (with Thorlacius and Uglum, 1993), the holographic principle (’t Hooft, 1993; Susskind, 1994), and fast scrambling (with Sekino, 2008). It is not by him and not endorsed by him, and where the field has not settled — which is most of the last chapter — the notes say so. Contents
    Symbol guide

    The quantities this book keeps returning to. Nothing here is more than one line of arithmetic; the hard part is always which entropy is meant.

    Two different entropies

    Sfine
    Fine-grained (von Neumann) entropy−tr ρ log ρ. Zero for a state you know exactly. Unitary evolution never changes it. This is the one that must come back down.
    Scoarse
    Coarse-grained (thermodynamic) entropyWhat you get after throwing away detail you cannot resolve. It rises when things mix, and it is allowed to. Most arguments that information is lost quietly swap this one in for the other.

    The black hole

    SBH
    Bekenstein–Hawking entropyS = A / 4G in units where ħ = c = k = 1, which is one quarter of the horizon's area in Planck areas. Bekenstein, 1972–73; the factor of a quarter, Hawking, 1975.
    A
    Horizon areaA = 16πG²M² for a non-rotating, uncharged hole. It scales as the square of the mass, so the entropy does too.
    ℓP
    Planck length√(ħG/c³) ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻³⁵ m. A Planck area is ℓP², and the horizon holds about one bit per four of them.
    T, β
    Hawking temperature, and its inverseT = ħc³/8πGMk; β = 1/T. Big holes are cold: a solar-mass hole is about 60 nanokelvin.
    t*
    Scrambling timeHow long until a dropped bit is spread over the whole horizon: about β log S. Sekino and Susskind, 2008.
    tPage
    Page timeWhen the radiation's fine-grained entropy peaks, roughly when the hole has given up about half its entropy. Page, 1993.

    Quantum states

    |Ψ⟩
    A pure stateEverything there is to know about a system. Its fine-grained entropy is exactly zero.
    ρ
    A density matrixWhat one part of a pure state looks like on its own. Entangle two halves and each half, looked at alone, appears mixed and hot even though the whole is pure.
    U
    Unitary evolutionThe rule that quantum mechanics runs by. It is invertible: run U backward and you are exactly where you started. "Information is not lost" is the claim that gravity obeys it too.
    Sgen
    Generalized entropyArea/4G + Sbulk. Extremize it over candidate surfaces and take the smallest answer: the rule behind the island formula.
