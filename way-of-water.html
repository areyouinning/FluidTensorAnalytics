    Symbol guide

    One flow carries most of these pages: a sheared layer of water with a row of slow eddies in it, set swaying. The symbols below are the ones the notes use to talk about it.

    The flow

    x, y
    Positionx runs along the layer and repeats every 2π, so the left and right edges of a plate are the same water. y runs across it.
    u, v
    Velocityu along x, v along y. Here u = y and v = −sin x + ε sin ωt.
    ψ, H₀
    Stream functionψ = y²/2 − cos x for the steady flow; u = ∂ψ/∂y, v = −∂ψ/∂x. Water moves along curves of constant ψ. The notes call the same quantity H₀ when they treat it as the energy of a pendulum.
    ε
    Sway amplitudeHow hard the layer is pushed side to side. ε = 0 is steady water.
    ω, T
    Sway frequency, periodT = 2π/ω. These pages use ω = 1.5, so T ≈ 4.19.
    t₀
    PhaseWhere in the sway cycle a measurement starts.

    Looking once per period

    P
    Period mapTakes a drop's position at the start of a sway to its position one period later. Also called the stroboscopic or Poincaré map. It preserves area because water is incompressible.
    p
    Hyperbolic fixed pointThe saddle, as P sees it: a point P returns to itself, with one direction that stretches and one that squeezes.
    λ
    Stretching factor or rateFor P at p, the factor by which the stretching direction grows each period (here about 66). For a steady saddle, the rate in e^(λt).
    Wᵘ
    Unstable manifoldEvery point that came from p, run backward far enough. Drawn in vermilion.
    Wˢ
    Stable manifoldEvery point that goes to p, run forward far enough. Drawn in blue.
    M(t₀)
    Melnikov functionTo first order in ε, the signed gap between Wᵘ and Wˢ, times the flow speed. Where M has a simple zero, the two curves cross.
    sech
    Hyperbolic secantsech z = 1 / cosh z. Falls off like 2e^(−z).

    Stirring and mixing

    σ, τ
    Finite-time Lyapunov exponentσ is how fast neighbours separate over a window of length τ, on a log scale, per unit time.
    Γ
    CirculationThe strength of a stirrer, modelled as a point vortex.
    a
    Cup radiusLengths in the cup chapters are in units of a.
    κ
    DiffusivityHow fast molecules spread a dissolved substance on their own. Sugar in water: about 5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s.
    Pe
    Péclet numberStirring speed times size, divided by κ. Large when stirring beats diffusion.
    ℓ_B
    Batchelor scaleThe layer thickness at which diffusion across a layer keeps pace with the stretching that thins it, about √(κ/λ).
