Hydrograph Modular

A modular rack where streamflow is a voltage source. The hydrograph module turns a water year into an envelope, a slow flow signal and a baseflow signal. The storm module drops random storms into a linear reservoir. The drum module turns rain into a beat: each storm is a hit, sized by how much it raised the river. The bottom row is a rainfall-runoff model you can patch: losses, a Nash cascade and Muskingum channel routing, built as filters.

New: the runoff row and the Flood routing patch. Pick a patch, then power on.

Patching

Drag from any jack to another. Each input takes one cable, and an output can feed several inputs. To move a cable, drag its plug off an input; drop it on bare panel to remove it. You can also tap one jack and then another, or focus a jack and press Enter on each end.

To turn a knob, drag up or down. Hold Shift for fine control, double-click to reset, or focus it and use the arrow keys. KEYS plays from Z S X D C V G B H N J M.

Signals

One cable type carries both audio and control voltage. Pitch inputs follow 1 V/oct: a signal of 1 raises pitch one octave. Envelopes and flows run from 0 to 1.

Jacks ringed in amber carry gates. A gate is both a timed event and a 0/1 signal. Gate inputs accept only gate outputs, but a gate output can patch into any input. Labels on solid tabs are outputs.

Gates from RAIN and STORM TRIG also carry the size of the event, from 0 to 1. DRUMS reads that as how hard to hit.

Hydrology in the rack

  • ENV is the hydrograph's fitted ADSR. Attack is the rising limb, decay τ is the peak recession, sustain is median baseflow divided by peak, and release τ is the recession constant k. ENV RATE converts days to seconds.
  • FLOW and BASE loop the whole water year over YEAR seconds. They are scaled by the annual peak (LIN) or on a log scale between the year's minimum and peak (LOG). Baseflow is separated with a Lyne–Hollick filter.
  • PEAK fires a gate each time the loop passes the annual peak.
  • STORM rains Poisson storms with exponentially distributed depths. RUNOFF is a linear reservoir: storage jumps by each depth, then drains as e−t/K.
  • SVF is Chamberlin's state-variable filter, run 2× oversampled.
  • RAIN fires a gate at the start of each storm in the water year: a day when quickflow (flow minus baseflow) starts rising by more than 1% of the year's range per day. The gate carries that storm's total rise as a fraction of the year's largest. Snowmelt years give a few big hits; flashy rain gives many small ones.
  • LOSS is the φ index: everything below φ soaks in, the rest runs off.
  • NASH is J. E. Nash's cascade of N equal linear reservoirs. A linear reservoir with time constant k is exactly a one-pole low-pass filter, so the module is N of them in series. Its response to a pulse of rain is a gamma curve, peaking at (N − 1)·k, drawn on the screen.
  • MUSKINGUM is G. T. McCarthy's 1938 channel routing, O = c₀I + c₁I₋₁ + c₂O₋₁, run at the audio sample rate as a first-order filter. X = 0 is a pure reservoir; X = 0.5 passes the wave on unchanged.
  • NASH and MUSKINGUM run in an AudioWorklet in double precision, so their gain at zero frequency is exactly 1: they reshape a flood in time without losing water. (The browser's built-in IIR filter node loses a large share of the volume at audio rates, so it is only a fallback.) BASEFLOW scales the result and adds steady groundwater flow, and FLOOD SCOPE plots rain against flow over the last few seconds. The Flood routing patch wires storms through all of it and lets the flow bend the pitch.
  • DRUMS plays the kick for storms at or above SPLIT and the snare for smaller ones, and a bigger storm rings longer. QUANT snaps each storm to the nearest CLOCK step. HAT plays a closed hat whose level follows HAT CV; patch BASE there and the hats swell with baseflow. The snare's two tones are the (0,1) and (1,1) modes of a 14-inch head; the hat is the TR-808's six square-wave oscillators.
