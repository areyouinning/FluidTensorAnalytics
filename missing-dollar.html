Riddles

Where did the missing dollar go?

Three guests take a room billed at $30 and pay $10 each. The clerk realises the room is only $25 and sends the bellhop up with $5. The $5 won’t split three ways, so each guest pockets $1 and the bellhop keeps $2. Now each guest has paid $9 — that’s $27 — and the bellhop has $2. Twenty-seven plus two is twenty-nine. Where is the thirtieth dollar?

Follow the money

Step through it and watch the whole $30 the entire time. It never leaves: it only moves between three pockets — the hotel’s, the bellhop’s, and the guests’ own, as their refund comes back.

Where’s the trick?

In the word plus. The riddle adds the bellhop’s $2 to the guests’ $27 — but the $2 is already inside that $27. What the guests paid breaks down as $25 to the hotel and $2 to the bellhop: twenty-five plus two is twenty-seven. Adding the $2 a second time counts it twice, and of course two numbers that overlap don’t add up to anything meaningful.

There are two honest ways to total the money, and both land on $30. Group it by where it ended up: $25 hotel + $2 bellhop + $3 back in pockets = $30. Or group it around what the guests paid: $27 paid + $3 refunded = $30. The only sum that misses is $27 + $2, because that pair was never supposed to be added — it’s a bill and a tip-that’s-part-of-the-bill, double-booked.

No dollar is missing. The riddle adds the bellhop’s $2 where the guests’ $3 refund belongs: to climb from the $27 paid back up to the original $30, you add the $3 that came back — not the $2 that stayed in the bellhop’s pocket. Twenty-seven plus two lands at twenty-nine, short of thirty by exactly $3 − $2 = $1. That gap between the refund and the tip is the dollar it dares you to find.

An old chestnut, in print since at least the 1930s. The arithmetic is exact and the diagram is mine.
