    For grown-ups

    Kiddo’s Monster Rally is a three-lap monster-truck race against three computer drivers. The truck drives itself forward; the player steers with the big arrow buttons, the arrow keys, or A and D. Driving on the grass slows you down, and soft walls keep everyone near the track.

    Every power-up is a question. Drive through a star crate and the race stops for one math or reading question. Answer it and you get a power-up: Nitro (a burst of speed), a Mud ball (slows the truck ahead), a Bubble (blocks one mud ball) or a Spring (a big jump). Tap the round button to use it.

    How it teaches. The questions follow direct instruction, the explicit teaching method developed by Siegfried Engelmann. A new skill starts with My turn: the game says and shows the answer. Then Your turn: the child answers. A mistake is corrected at once: the game models the answer, asks the same question again straight away, and asks it once more a couple of questions later. Five right answers in a row on the first try move the child up a level; if more than half of the recent answers are wrong, the game steps back a level. About one question in four reviews a level already mastered, so old skills stay fresh.

    Math runs from counting to 5, through adding and taking away within 10 and adding within 20, to the times tables. Reading runs from letter sounds and first sounds, to reading short words and matching them to pictures, finding a spoken word among look-alikes, and sight words. The letter and word sounds are the same recordings as Sound It Out; other prompts use your device’s own voice.

    Saving. Stars, the truck, unlocked paints and stickers, and each subject’s level are kept on this device only, in the browser’s storage. Stars come from first-try answers and from finishing places, and unlock new paints, stickers and wheels in the garage.

    For older or younger players. Set the rivals’ speed on the subject screen. To start a subject at a different level, or to clear everything, use these buttons.
