Paradox

Can Achilles catch the tortoise?

Achilles runs ten times as fast as a tortoise, which starts 100 meters ahead. By the time Achilles reaches where the tortoise was, it has moved on 10 meters. When he covers those, it has moved 1 more, and so on forever. Zeno said he can never catch it. He catches it in about 11.1 seconds.

Try it

Press Zeno step to run one stage at a time: each ends when Achilles reaches the spot where the tortoise stood at the start of it. Then press Just run to let time flow normally.

The answer

The gap closes at 10 − 1 = 9 meters every second, so Achilles catches the tortoise after 100 / 9 = 11.11… seconds. By then he has run 111.11… meters and the tortoise 11.11… meters.

Where the argument goes wrong

Zeno is right that there are infinitely many stages. But they take less and less time: 10 + 1 + 0.1 + 0.01 + … seconds. Infinitely many pieces can add up to a finite total. This is a geometric series, a / (1 − r) = 10 / (1 − 0.1) = 11.11…, and it is the same answer as the simple division above.

Modern calculus settles the arithmetic. Philosophers still debate whether it fully answers Zeno’s worry about completing infinitely many tasks. The lesson here is narrower: an infinite list of steps is not the same as an infinite amount of time.

Zeno of Elea posed this and related paradoxes of motion in the 5th century BCE; we know them mostly through Aristotle’s Physics. The words and diagram are mine.
