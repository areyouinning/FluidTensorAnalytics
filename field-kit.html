Flying and reading

Fly
W S A D or arrows move. Q climbs, E sinks; the wheel or a pinch does too. Shift is three times faster. Drag to look. Speed scales with height, so high up you cross a state in seconds. G lands.
Click
In Fly, a feature opens its card and bare ground flies you there. Probe reads elevation, every layer under the point, and on request the mapped geology. Measure adds points: distance, area and an elevation profile.
Layers
Each layer colors, sizes, extrudes, labels and filters by any of its columns. A date column wakes the clock at the bottom. Line features can be ridden from their card, or from a measured path.
What the ground is
Baked files, not the network: AWS Terrain Tiles (USGS 3DEP) to z13, about 15 m per pixel, and NAIP aerial imagery to z17, about 0.9 m, for one box from Moab to Paradox Valley. Outside that box there is nothing to draw. A tile cell that straddles a narrow canyon reads high and a sharp summit reads low, so elevations are for looking, not for design.
Offline

Where your data goes
Visits and photos are saved in this browser only, and leave it only when you export them. Nothing is uploaded.
Privacy
Dropped files never leave this tab and are gone on reload. Tile servers see where you look. Search and each live source send the place you asked about to that service.
