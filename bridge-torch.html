Riddles

Can four people cross in 17 minutes?

Four people reach a narrow bridge at night. It holds two at a time, and they have one torch, which must go with anyone crossing. They walk at different speeds: 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes to cross. A pair walks at the slower one’s pace. Can they all get over in 17 minutes?

Try it

Tap one or two walkers on the torch’s bank, then press Cross. Someone has to bring the torch back each time. When you are stuck, press Show the best plan.

The obvious plan

Let the fastest walker escort everyone. The 1 and the 2 go over (2 minutes), the 1 comes back (1), the 1 and the 5 go (5), the 1 comes back (1), the 1 and the 10 go (10). That is 2 + 1 + 5 + 1 + 10 = 19 minutes. Press Greedy plan to watch it.

The trick

Send the two slowest across together, so the 10-minute walk also covers the 5. The 1 and 2 cross (2), the 1 returns (1), the 5 and 10 cross (10), the 2 returns (2), and the 1 and 2 cross again (2). The total is 2 + 1 + 10 + 2 + 2 = 17. Sending the 2 back costs more than sending the 1 back, yet the plan wins.

Why 17 is the best

I checked every possible schedule with a shortest-path search over the states (who is on which bank, and where the torch is). For 1, 2, 5 and 10 the minimum is 17, so no plan beats it.

For four people there is a simple rule. Call the two fastest x ≤ y and the second slowest s. The pairing plan beats the greedy one exactly when 2y < x + s; here 2·2 = 4 is less than 1 + 5 = 6. I tested this against the search on all 1,365 four-person sets with times up to 12. In every one, the best answer was the better of those two plans. With 1, 2, 3, 4 the two tie at 11.

The lesson: the greedy choice is locally sensible and globally worse. Good plans sometimes spend time to save more.
