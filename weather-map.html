This one moved

The forecast and the radar now live inside Wind where you actually are, next to the terrain they belong to. You will be sent there in a moment.

Why, in full

This page had two problems and only one of them was visible.

The visible one: it drew its radar frames from RainViewer's free feed, which used to include a short forecast loop alongside the past frames. That loop is no longer published, so the array came back empty and the page read the first item of nothing. It crashed before drawing a single map.

The one that mattered more: the forecast on this page was never a forecast. The temperature, dew point, wind and precipitation curves were generated in the browser from sine waves and a random number generator, and four of the snow-water markers were invented figures pinned to the names of real Colorado passes. It looked like data. It was a drawing of what data looks like.

A page that does that is worse than a page that is down, so rather than repair it the two honest parts moved. The radar is real and is now drawn over your own terrain square. The forecast is now the one the wind twin was already fetching — Open-Meteo, with the National Weather Service behind it — shown as a plain two-day chart.
