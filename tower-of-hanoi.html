Riddles

How many moves to move the tower?

Three pegs. A stack of disks sits on the first, largest at the bottom. Move the whole stack to the last peg. Move one disk at a time, and never put a larger disk on a smaller one. How few moves does it take?

Try it

Tap a peg to lift its top disk, then tap another peg to set it down. Try three disks first, then add more.

The recursive idea

To move n disks, first move the top n − 1 out of the way to the spare peg. Move the biggest disk to the target. Then move the n − 1 back on top of it. Each of those smaller moves is the same puzzle with one fewer disk. The biggest disk moves exactly once.

Counting the moves

Let T(n) be the fewest moves for n disks. The recipe gives T(n) = 2·T(n − 1) + 1, with T(1) = 1. Solving it gives T(n) = 2n − 1: 7 moves for 3 disks, 15 for 4, 31 for 5.

That is the minimum, not just one way. The biggest disk cannot move until the other n − 1 are all on one other peg, and it cannot rest until they have moved again. So at least 2·T(n − 1) + 1 moves are needed.

A pattern in binary

Number the moves 1, 2, 3, and so on. Write the move number in binary and find its lowest 1. If that is the last digit, disk 1 moves. If it is the next digit, disk 2 moves, and so on. So the smallest disk moves on every odd move, disk 2 on every fourth move starting from move 2, and the biggest disk exactly once, in the middle.

The legend

Édouard Lucas sold the puzzle in 1883 with a story of priests moving 64 golden disks. At one move a second, 264 − 1 moves would take about 585 billion years.
