Riddles

More wine in the water, or water in the wine?

Two glasses hold the same amount, one of wine and one of water. Take a spoonful of wine, put it in the water, and stir (or do not). Then take a spoonful of that mixture and put it back in the wine. Is there now more wine in the water glass, or more water in the wine glass?

Try it

Make your guess first. Then spoon the wine over, spoon the mixture back, and watch the two readouts. Change the spoon and the stirring and do it again.

The answer

They are exactly equal. Whatever the size of the spoon, and however well or badly you stir, the amount of wine in the water glass is the amount of water in the wine glass.

Why, without algebra

Each glass ends with the same volume it started with. The spoon took out some amount and later put the same amount back.

So look at the wine glass. Whatever wine is missing from it has been replaced, milliliter for milliliter, by something else. The only other liquid around is water. The missing wine is not lost; the only place it can be is the water glass. So the wine in the water glass equals the water in the wine glass.

Notice that the argument never asks what the spoon picked up the second time.

A number check

Take 200 ml glasses, a 20 ml spoon and a thorough stir. After the first spoon the water glass holds 220 ml, of which 20 ml is wine. A 20 ml spoonful of that carries 20 × 20/220 ≈ 1.82 ml of wine and about 18.18 ml of water back to the wine glass. That leaves 18.18 ml of wine in the water glass, and 18.18 ml of water in the wine glass. Equal, as promised.

Now set the stirring to 0%. In this model the spoon then scoops back only the wine it just put in, so both amounts are zero. They are still equal.

The lesson

This is a conservation argument. It works because you only ask where things ended up, not how they got there.
